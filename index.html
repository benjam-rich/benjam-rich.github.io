Research

"Science and everyday life cannot and should not be separated." -Rosalind Franklin

The capabilities of AI have advanced faster than our understanding of how people use them.

I study human-centered AI augmentation — how people think, decide, and behave when working with intelligent systems, and how the human side of that interaction shapes what augmentation ultimately delivers. My research advances a broader vision of interpretable, effective, and responsible AI use, organized around three interrelated threads.

User Cognition

Individual differences in cognition — heuristics, efficacy beliefs, attributions, and traits like divergent thinking — shape how each user approaches, interprets, and acts when using intelligent systems.

Decision-Making with AI

Many organizational decisions are made in collaboration with AI agents. AI reshapes the process by which decisions are made.

Humanistic AI Use

Understanding the boundaries of responsible AI use has never been more pressing. I ground my research in a humanistic commitment — intelligent systems should amplify, rather than erode, human capacities, character, and conscience.
